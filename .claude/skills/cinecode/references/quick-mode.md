# Quick mode

The whole film lives in one file, `film.html`: the timeline, every scene as a function of time, the words, and the soundtrack (Web Audio, rendered offline). The main chat does everything; `make.py` renders, checks and answers in a few lines. For a showcase with per-shot approval and layered sound, use Film mode (`film-mode.md`).

The work runs on three habits: **a short, exact plan** (one card with seconds, colours and sizes), **your own eyes before anything long renders** (stills at the key moments, compared with the card), and **notes as numbers** (what changes, by how much).

## Flow

| # | Step | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Questions in one round (+ voice when a narrator is chosen) | answers |
| 1b | Research in the background, when chosen | shown at 2 |
| 2 | The production card, as text, within a minute or two | OK, one note or a swap |
| 3 | The look and two scenes only, as stills | go on, or a note (none for a complete brief) |
| 3b | The rest in passes; their stills go out as progress | none |
| 4 | Half-size draft | final, notes or a jury check |
| 5 | Up to two revision rounds on drafts, then the final | OK |

Setup and the machine check: `SKILL.md`, setup.

## 1. Questions (one round)

**What the user already said is never asked again.** A brief that settles a question (the idea, the length and format, the look, the sound: "a 20-second film with no words") skips it; the medium is the one question asked even then, the brief's choice as its ★ (1a). Leaving it to you ("gerisi senin kararın", "you decide", "kendin seç", "beni şaşırt", "surprise me") settles nothing: the ideas question (after the prediction test, the sparks and the overlap check) and the style board with its question still come, each one short question with the ★ first, since the idea and the look are where the user's taste enters; a surprise lives in what the options offer, never in skipping the choice; a complete brief goes straight to the card, shown in one message while the build starts at once: the draft is its first gate (`SKILL.md`, A complete brief). The card keeps the user's own concrete images in their own words, never thinned into general ones. A medium or look the brief names (linocut, watercolour, ukiyo-e, a director) still brings its craft even with no board: `styles.py suggest "<the brief>"` names the matching recipe; read that recipe's essence and rules before writing the card (a linocut is black block with white carved out of it, not flat shapes on white paper). A measured song or reference video (`music-video.md`, `reference-video.md`) answers some questions the same way (length and format, style, sound); skip those unless the user wants them changed. With the question tool: **call 1** (after the mode and purpose call) = idea, medium, length and format, sound (when the first message asked for a narrator, its voice type takes sound's place and sound moves to call 2); then the style board is built for the interpretation and the medium the user picked (seconds) and it is sent to the user (`SendUserFile`, render) with one line; **call 2** = the style question first, alone about the board, then references and music, plus voice type and language when a narrator is chosen (four questions a call: what does not fit goes to a third). The style never shares a call with the idea: a board seen beside other questions reads as if the options were about it. Each question with ★ and a few words why, and the user's own answer.

1. **Fikir / Idea.** Three steps before the question, each catching what the maker cannot see in its own ideas (`writing.md` §1). **The prediction test**, started as soon as the film's folder exists (right after `init_film.py`, so the helper writes into the film), where the tool has helpers: `python3 tools/brief.py predict --task "<the user's first message, quoted>"` prints the helper's brief; start a helper with it (model as printed), and it writes `research/predict.md`. **The sparks,** meanwhile: `python3 tools/deal.py deal spark` (before the film's folder exists, `python3 KIT/scripts/deal.py deal spark --project <film>`) deals three hands within a few seconds, each led by a seed, a real thing drawn by chance from outside (a museum object, a species, something in space, an invention, a heritage site, a craft or custom; each hand from another pool), with a place, a form, a medium and two more cards (an engine, a tone, an era, a colour) from about 480, the cards of your last films left out. Each candidate crosses the user's idea with a different hand: the idea stays at the centre and takes what its seed holds (a shape, a material, a motion, a sound, its story), never a copy of it; a seed is never dropped for being unfamiliar, only by a redeal with its reason. **The overlap check:** the best five as candidates in `research/candidates.md`, then `python3 tools/brief.py overlap --task "<the user's first message, quoted>"`, a helper (about half a minute) that marks each CLEAR, SHARES (one element repeats) or OVERLAP (the idea itself repeats) against the predictions and this user's own films. The guard holds the ideas question until all three are done. Then 2–3 interpretations that are not OVERLAP (a SHARES one with its shared element changed), read against `steering.md`, each saying in a few words what it does that the predictions and the user's past films did not: ★ the reading that lands (the clearest and most surprising one, its shape taken from its own story); the user's literal version stays an option with its honest cost; the pick is logged (`deal.py pick spark s2 --reason "…"`).
1a. **Biçim / Medium,** in call 1 beside the idea, always asked: what the film is made of, chosen before anything is built inside it (the board's rows, the card's code fit, the renderer). The 3–4 media that suit the user's idea, ★ first with a few words why, each one line of what the viewer sees and its honest cost: 3D realistic (real light and materials, like live action; the longest to build, above all with several worlds) · 2D animation (cel, rubber hose, anime, flat graphic) · hand-made (paint, ink, cut paper, clay or a maquette) · motion design (type, shapes and diagrams that move) · pixel or a retro screen; and the user's own answer. A brief or a named look that points to one (a Ghibli film, a woodcut, "3B", "çizgi film olsun") makes it the ★ ("senin dediğin"), the others beside it; only a complete brief goes without the question. The answer goes into `STATE.md` (`- **Medium:** <in the user's words>`) before the board; the guard holds the board until it is there.
2. **Süre ve format / Length and format.** 15 s · 30 s 9:16 · 60 s · 1–3 min 16:9 · 1:1 · own; the ★ follows the idea (`steering.md` §2).
3. **Stil ve his / Style and mood.** The code-fit check first (`looks.md` §5). Your own eye leads; the library is a source of ideas, never the limit. Plus three mood words; the ★ follows the purpose file §1.
   - **A look the user named** (a director, film, studio, artist or era: "Wes Anderson gibi", "Ghibli", "Villeneuve", "Vox"): you know that look better than any recipe. Name what makes it (Wes Anderson: frontal symmetric frames, pastel sets, dollhouse cross-sections, centred type, whip pans) and offer 3–4 readings of it for this idea, your own, each one line of what the viewer sees and how code draws it. **Every reading gets pictures:** pick the film of that look that carries each reading, search four stills from across it (`asset_search.py search --q "<film title>" --sources filmgrab --n 4 --out research/look-<n>`; a title gives stills from across that one film), Write the look in `STATE.md` first (`- **Look named:** <the name>`), and for a film that informs make one reading led by motion design (`motion-design.md`: its devices named by job). Put them on one board, one row per reading, numbered like the options: `python3 tools/direction.py board --row "1|<reading>||research/look-1" --row "2|…||research/look-2" … --out research/readings.jpg` (each row: four stills and its measured palette). The stills are mood only. A library recipe joins only when it truly fits (`styles.py suggest` names the matches and says so; its id goes in the row's third field and brings its poster), never to fill a slot.
   - **Never ask before the user has seen the pictures:** the board is sent (`SendUserFile`, render) right before the style question; a question that mentions a board the user has not been given is a broken gate.
   - **No look named:** the board is mostly authored, not the library, and every row sits inside the chosen medium: at least three rows are directions authored for this idea from that medium's own sources (3D realistic: films and cinematographers; 2D animation: animated films and studios; hand-made: painters, illustrators and crafts; motion design: title sequences and explainers), starting from the six references `python3 tools/deal.py deal look` deals from about 190 (the three that serve this idea in this medium; a hand with fewer: `deal.py redeal look --reason "medium: …"`), each with its stills (films and animated films: `asset_search.py search --q "<film title or cat:<cinematographer>>" --sources filmgrab --n 4 --max-wait 12`; paintings, prints and crafts: `--sources aic,cleveland,met`, every row started at once as its own background shell call (`run_in_background`), about 20 s together; a row that finds nothing becomes a library recipe, never a second round of searching), drawn as one image with `direction.py board`, the whole board within about 90 s, each row labelled with the number and the name its option carries in the style question (`--row "1|<the option's name>||research/look-1"`), so the user finds the row the option names; library recipes fill the other rows (a recipe row brings its poster), never the whole board; a film that informs gets one row led by motion design (`motion-design.md`: its devices named by job); the rows differ in tone too, at least one in daylight or in colour (`direction.py board` says ONE TONE). For the library rows, `python3 tools/styles.py suggest "<the idea in the user's words>" --board research/style-board.jpg` gives six different candidates (families, eras, materials; never six shades of one) on a numbered board; keep the ones in the chosen medium. Swap a weak one for a better recipe (`styles.py board <1> … <6>`) or for a look you invent for this idea (one line, no poster); never swap in a style the user saw lately (`styles.py suggest` moves them back and names them; `styles.py recent` lists them), and never reach for the same few safe ones by habit; a board that feels narrow gets a second `styles.py suggest` with other words (a material, an era, a mood). The question names the board ("Panodaki 6 stilden hangisi?"); the options are the ★ and the strongest alternatives (as many as the tool allows: four in Claude Code), each starting with its board number and one line of what it looks like ("1 · Rubber Hose: mürekkep çizgili 1930'lar çizgi filmi"); the question's description says the rest are picked by typing their number in "Other" ("5 ya da 6 için numarasını yaz").
   - **"None of these: find a more creative direction"** is always the last option of the style question. On it, the library is set aside: write three directions authored for this idea alone, inside the chosen medium (another medium is the user's own answer), each from a different source (a film or a director's look, a craft or material, an art movement or a photographer), each one line of what the viewer sees and how code draws it, none of them a recipe or a family the user saw on the board. Give each its pictures exactly as for a named look (four stills or artworks per row, `direction.py board`), send the board and ask again with those three and the user's own answer.
   - **Boards and stills are shown big:** the tool prints "show the user: <path>"; in Claude Code send it with `SendUserFile` (`display: "render"`), which opens it big in the side panel; elsewhere put the page it names in the message. Reading the image yourself is for your own eyes: the user sees only a tiny thumbnail of it.
4. **Ses / Sound.** a) narrator + music · b) music + effects, no words · c) on-screen text + music · d) own, including the user's song (`music-video.md`). ★ Ad → a; Cinematic → b; unsure → ask; never pick a voice-over silently.
   **Müzik / Music** (skipped for the user's own song): a) ★ a real open-licence recording, searched (`sound.md` §8, "A real recording"): real players and a real mix; the cuts follow its bars · b) written for this film (code and recorded players, `sound.md` §8) · c) own track · d) none, sound design only. It is its own question in the round, never folded into Sound (`SKILL.md`: The music is always the user's). The ★ is a), and stays a) in the question the user sees. Cutting on the beat is what a recording does best (`beat_grid.py` measures its bars and the cuts sit on them): beat sync, energy or a fast edit is never a reason for b). b) only when sound must follow single events note by note (a cartoon scored to its gags, a melody typed key by key) or one search found nothing that fits; its option says honestly that code scores share one small set of voices and sound alike from film to film.
5. **Referanslar / References.** a) ★ search: stills from films that look like ours, and real shapes where the subject is real · b) only mine (`refs/`, or a film or cinematographer's name) · c) skip (abstract films).

**Ad & product:** one plain line asks only what the user surely knows: the name, two or three things that make it different, and, if they have them, a logo, colours, the call to action or their footage. Never ask for hex codes or fact lists; derive the specificity facts (`purpose-ad.md` §3) and show them at the gate.

**A narrator:** asked once (skipped when the brief or an earlier call settled it): voice type (warm documentary · energetic · intimate first person · elderly storyteller · playful · deadpan; the three that suit, ★ first, the rest reachable as the own answer) and language (★ the user's). Check `keys.py list` before the gate; a missing key is one line at the gate (`keys.py open GEMINI_API_KEY`).

Example (Türkçe):

> **1. Fikir:** a) ★ … b) … c) … · kendi cevabın
> **1a. Biçim:** a) ★ … (neden) · b) 3B gerçekçi (gerçek ışık ve malzeme; kurması en uzun süren, birkaç dünya varsa daha da) · c) 2B animasyon · d) el yapımı (boya, mürekkep, kâğıt, kil) · e) hareketli grafik · kendi cevabın
> **2. Süre/format:** a) ★ … (neden) · b) … · c) … · kendi cevabın
> **3. Stil + his:** (panoda numaralı) a) ★ 1 · … (neden) · b) 2 · … · diğerleri için numarasını yaz; üç kelime: …
> **4. Ses:** a) ★ … (neden) · b) … · c) … · kendi cevabın
> **4b. Müzik:** a) ★ gerçek bir açık lisanslı kayıt (aranır) · b) bu film için yazılır · c) kendi parçan · d) yok, yalnızca ses tasarımı
> **5. Referanslar:** a) ★ filmimize benzeyen filmlerden kareler, gerçek bir yer ya da nesne varsa onun fotoğrafları · b) sadece benim referanslarım · c) atla

Under the questions, one line: the sound identity you picked for this story and the defaults (hook from frame 1, on-screen text allowed, no subtitles unless asked, −14 LUFS).

## 1b. Research in the background (when chosen)

**Every claim about a real person or event has its source** (`writing.md` §16: `research/facts.md`, one row per claim, unconfirmed ones cut or said as such). **First the supply probe, for a real person, team, event or brand** (seconds, nothing downloaded): `python3 tools/asset_search.py supply --q "<element>" --q "<element>" …` counts what the open archives hold for each key element (about 20 s; run it beside the board's searches, never before them). Tell the user the numbers at once: where they are thin or empty (recent events, private collections, agency photos) searching longer finds nothing, so offer the routes then: their own licensed material in `assets/`, a design-led film (type, numbers, maps, recreated places, silhouettes), or a mix. **A search round is boxed:** at most 15 minutes, helpers included; a deeper round is the user's call, with the probe's numbers in front of them.

Start every search at once in the background (the shell tool's `run_in_background`) and write the card meanwhile; the user never waits on the network. Two kinds, never confused: **how the film looks** comes from real films; **what a real thing looks like** comes from open archives, which never set the look.

1. **Look references, always** (unless c): 2–3 films or cinematographers the card points to, the user's own names first ("Villeneuve" → *Blade Runner 2049*, *Sicario*, or `cat:Roger Deakins`), 1–2 stills each:
   ```bash
   python3 tools/asset_search.py search --q "<film title>" --sources filmgrab --n 6 --out research/look-<a>
   python3 tools/asset_search.py search --q "cat:<cinematographer or director>" --sources filmgrab --n 3 --out research/look-<b>
   ```
   FilmGrab stills are mood only: copyrighted, never in the film or git; the first stills are compared with them side by side.
   **Pick films by their light and frame, never by their topic:** a film about the same topic is not a reference for how this frame must look. Ask what the frame must look like (its light, its colour, its framing) and name films whose pictures look like that. A title search takes stills from across that one film (a wrong or missing title says so; fix it); search 6 and keep the 2–3 that show the light you mean, dropping faces and wide shots that say nothing about it (`board --drop look-<a>:1,4`). A `cat:` search gives one still from each of that person's films, whatever they look like; prefer titles. When the look lives in ads or product films rather than features (macro products, food, watches), FilmGrab has little: say so in one line, and let the style's poster and the subject references carry the look.
2. **Subject references, only for real places, objects, costumes or creatures:** the key elements, counted by length (`craft.md` §0: 2–3 for 15–30 s, 3–5 for 60 s, 2 per chapter beyond), one search each, English, the shape in the words (the part of the thing that is seen, never only the place it is in):
   ```bash
   python3 tools/asset_search.py search --q "<element>" --sources wikimedia,met,aic --n 2 --out research/<element>
   ```
   (`aic`, `cleveland` or `rijks` for artworks, `nasa` for space.) Drop off-topic results, tourist snapshots, duplicates, another brand's product (a real brand's watch, car or bottle is someone else's design), illustrations and museum pieces from another era unless the film is about them (`board --drop <folder>:<numbers>`); a weak board is worse than a short one.
3. **One board,** look references first, with `refs/` (the same picture twice is left out by itself); read only its summary lines (one per reference and a measured palette), never single images or the full JSON:
   ```bash
   python3 tools/asset_search.py board --dirs research/look-<a> research/look-<b> research/<element> --per 3 --refs refs --out research/board-v1
   ```
4. Build from them in code: silhouettes, proportions, key details, the palette; real places and objects follow their references where accuracy matters. An image enters the film only when its licence allows and `asset_search.py fetch` wrote its `LICENSES.csv` row.

At the gate, "swap 3" runs one more search for that element; "daha fazla referans" runs one more round on the elements named. Neither uses a revision round.

## 2. The production card (one text gate)

Fast: within a minute or two of the answers, as text; nothing heavy (scene code, renders) runs before the user has seen it.

First the **writer pass**, privately (`writer-prompt.md`): diverge, discard the obvious, the turn when the story has one, ending first, show don't tell, the word budget (narration ≈ runtime × 4/3 words, ≤ 9 a line; cards ≤ 6 words), the banned tells (`writing.md` §6), cut 30 %, and every line read as a native speaker of the film's language would say it (no list of bare verbs, no coined or translated-sounding phrase). Narration lines add what the picture cannot show (`writing.md` §4, §9).

**A film of several worlds** (a journey, a montage, chapters in different places) takes each world from its own seed: `python3 tools/deal.py deal seed --n <worlds>` deals one real thing per world, each from another pool; each world is built from what its seed holds, never a copy of it; the worlds the user named stay theirs; the pick is logged (`deal.py pick seed all --reason "…"`).

Then one message:

- **Logline:** what moves in this film, a change or, for a mood piece or a portrait, what the viewer is left with; and the turn, when the story has one.
- **Merak / Curiosity,** one line: the question the viewer holds from the first 2 s (raised by what the picture holds back, never answered there), the second it is held back or turned, the second it is paid. A film of 30–60 s that informs or tells may write a chain of questions instead, each answer raising the next (`writing.md` §14); a cinematic, poetic or loop film keeps one question. Motion keeps the eye; only an open question keeps the viewer (`writing.md` §13).
- **Beats, second by second** (rows by length, `craft.md` §0: 4–8 for 30 s; by chapter over 60 s): time · beat type (`craft.md` §3) · what the subject does and what it changes · the camera line ("dolly in, 3 s, ease-in-out, toward the figure", `camera-moves.md`) · the transition · the word, line or sound on it.
- **Key moments** (2–4: the moments the film rests on, such as its thesis line, its hardest fact, its turn when it has one, its last line or image; `writing.md` §15), each with its sound moment.
- The **reference board,** when research ran.
- The **director card,** below.
- Type cards only when words carry the film (§2b).

One question call: "Kurayım mı ★ · bir not · bir değişiklik · daha fazla referans"; beside it the thesis line in 2–3 wordings that differ in verb or stance (a wordless film: the last image), and the type pick when words matter. Apply a note, then build; the card is kept in `STATE.md`.

**The director card** is the only place devices are chosen. Each line carries numbers where they exist and its reason from this story in a few words, and names things a stranger could draw from it: "cinematic", "moody", "atmospheric" or "textured" alone decide nothing; the user changes any line in plain words ("tempo daha yavaş", "baskı rengini aç"), applied before the draft without a new question.

- **Kod uyumu / Code fit:** three answers. **How each thing is seen:** one clause per kind of thing on screen (the subject, people, vehicles, buildings, the sky, the ground) in this film's medium: its shape, the part of it the light picks out, where we see it from. **The subject on screen:** how the subject itself is seen at the key moments (the animal itself, the real people's photographs, the landmark as it stands), recognisable at first glance; shapes, stand-in animals or a nearby topic never carry it. A real event, person or place: say where real material (archive photographs, footage, documents, `asset_search.py`) carries the film and where code or motion design does; real material where it testifies (the people, the place, the moment), code where it explains or where no picture exists; the mix is this film's choice, not a rule. **What would look cheap:** each realistic person, building, vehicle or product this film needs, and its route (`looks.md` §5, What reads as old CG); for each 3D world its renderer and the texture its main surfaces carry. "Low-poly", "flat-shaded" or no texture is not a route. A harder route (realistic people, 3D characters) is offered with its honest cost, never chosen silently.
- **Yapı / Structure:** what carries the film, in one sentence (a skeleton from `writing.md` §14 bent to the idea, or the film's own form).
- **Stil / Look:** the direction picked on the board (authored for this film, or a library recipe) and why.
- **Palet / Palette:** 4–6 hex values with their roles (ground, subject, accent, text), and how colour is laid in this medium (printed plates on the white, washes that bleed, glow from its sources, spill on nearby surfaces). The subject's colour survives the light; the family's untouched palette needs a reason.
- **Atmosfer / Atmosphere:** how each place is made a world rather than a drawing, decided from this story and this medium: its light (where it comes from, how hard, what it catches; `looks.md` §11), its depth (what is near, what is far), what leads the eye to the subject, and how the medium marks every surface. Weather, haze, night and a named lamp are choices of a story that needs them, never a default: a flat noon, a lit studio or a printed field is as much a world. One line for each place or world in the film, never one sentence for all. The subject's face or the product gets the light that makes it read.
- **Işığın hikâyesi / Light arc:** when the light or its colour changes across the film: how, what the change says and the seconds it changes; a film whose light holds says so.
- **Kurgu / Form:** one continuous shot or a cut film, from `python3 tools/moves.py form "<the brief>" --seconds N` and the story (`camera-moves.md`, One continuous shot): a journey, a pursuit, a change of scale or a reveal in one place is one shot, and then the Camera line is the path of that one move in beats; a film that lists, explains or travels between places is cut.
- **Kamera / Camera:** the view first: **perspective** (`VIEW`, a camera standing in a 3D world) whenever the camera looks along or around something (a road or track receding, a vehicle at three quarters, an orbit, a flight through a place); **flat planes** (`CAM`, `plane()`) only for frontal staging (titles, a side-on frieze, collage); three.js for lit 3D solids. Then the character in a few words (calm and held · flowing dolly and crane · handheld and close · locked-off graphic) and the signature move. Start from a drawn deck, not from habit: `python3 tools/moves.py deal --shots <n> --tempo <tempo> --seed "<the idea>"` deals moves, sizes, transitions and one big move weighted by the tempo, with no neighbours alike and the moves your last films opened or signed with pushed back; keep what serves each beat, swap the rest with a reason.
- **Hareket / Motion:** one rule per kind of thing: the subject (spring word firm · soft · lively · bouncy, `motion.md` §5, and what leads), the words (how they enter, how long they hold), the camera (how it starts and settles).
- **Tempo:** energetic (about 4 s a shot) · standard (about 6 s) · contemplative (8–12 s), the ★ from this story with a reason; then the numbers: average shot length, shot count (`craft.md` §0), BPM and cut rhythm (long holds · on the bar · on the beat). Choose the BPM so the key moments land on beats (a bar = 4 × 60 / BPM s). Over 60 s each chapter gets a tag (fast · breath · hold; never three alike in a row).
- **Geçişler / Transitions:** the kinds and where (cut, match, smash, dissolve, whip), from the same deck.
- **Font:** the ★ face or pairing and its reason (§2b).
- **Müzik / Music:** the genre from `sound.md` §8 and why it fits this story; a recording: what to search for (genre, players, tempo), then after the pick its BPM and the bars the key moments land on; a written score: BPM, key, progression, groove (its step pattern), lead instrument, arc (what enters when, the peak, the ending); never the slow drone-and-glockenspiel bed by habit, and not the instruments of your last films unless the film asks for them.
- **Ses / Sound:** the identity line; what makes a sound in this film (the 4–8 kinds of event its sound marks) and from what (a recording, a layered hit, a template voice); the bed under each place; with a narrator the voice treatment, medium in space and why (`sound.md` §4b), never a default whisper.
- **Son / Ending:** how it closes and why; never a scrolling list of sources.
- **Finish per scene and motion design:** the card names each scene's finishing devices (`finish.md`) and the motion design devices with the job each does, or none and why (`motion-design.md`); the first picture code waits for them.
- **Doku / Finishing:** the film's texture (its grain, its tone, anything in its air) as this story and medium want it, or none, with the reason. The three finishing passes (motion blur, print tone, gate weave) stay off unless the user turns one on from this line (motion blur: render time × 6–8 on those scenes) (`purpose-cinematic.md` §2).
- **İmza / Signature:** a move, motif, effect (`motion.md` §8) or sound that returns, changed, through the film; it marks the identity and never stands in for variety.
- **Kontrol / Checks:** the seconds the first stills will show (the key moments) and what each must prove ("yüz 9:16'da okunuyor", "başlık 3,2 sn'de kadraja sığıyor").

**Variety is the rule:** camera moves, shot sizes, light and type change as often as the tempo asks (energetic: a new move or size every 2–4 s; standard: about every 5–6 s; contemplative: long holds that keep revealing); adjacent shots never repeat a move or size, and never the same designed transition twice in a row (a plain cut is the default joint and may follow a cut); something new reaches the eye or the ear every few seconds. What stays constant is the identity: one visual language and the signature. A device is never added for its own sake; an idea that comes up during the build goes back to the card as a proposal. The libraries (58 styles, the light vocabulary, the shot catalog, the helpers) are vocabulary, never a checklist.

**Inserts** (a photograph, a document or a real clip cut into a drawn scene): an insert opens or closes its scene and holds at least 1.6 s; it never splits a scene so the drawn picture comes back after it (scene, photo, the same scene again reads as a repeat), and no shot is shorter than 0.6 s unless the card names a flash or a strobe. Before the draft, go through the inserts against the scene list: each one's start, end and the scene on either side.

### 2b. Type direction (only when words matter)

## 3. Build: one file

Start from `film.html` (or a saved format, §8). Learn the engine from `KIT/templates/quick/HELPERS.md` (every helper's signature, about 2 K tokens), not from the starter's code. **Delete the example scenes first** (`fieldGathers`, `ringSnaps`, `titleHolds`, their `SCENES`, `SFX` and `CUES` rows). Edit only the FILM section: `TITLE`, `PURPOSE`, `LOOKS`/`LOOK_ID`, `FINISH`, `DURATION`, `BPM`, `BEAT0`, `OWN_MUSIC`, `PALETTE`, `TYPE`, `CUES`, `VO`, `CAPTIONS`, `CAPTION_STYLE`, `SCENES`, `SFX`, `ROOM`, `ARRANGEMENT`, `INSTRUMENTS`, the scene functions and `score()`.

**In passes, one scene per edit. First the look and two scenes only** (about 150–250 lines): the opening and the scene least like it (another world, the hero, the hardest light), so a yes covers the film's range. Their stills, within minutes (a 60 s film's first look within about five), are a gate: sent (`SendUserFile`, render) with one question (★ devam · bir not · görünümü değiştir), and no other scene is written before the answer (with a complete brief they go out as progress and the build runs on to the draft). Then the rest three or four scenes at a time; the sound last. A pass that thinks for minutes before its first edit is too big: split it. Never rewrite the file whole.

**Look before you show:** `python3 tools/make.py stills --at <the Kontrol seconds>` writes `review/stills-N.jpg`. Read the sheet against the card's Kontrol line, the look references and the template check (nothing left of the example scenes; this story's subject, place and light in every frame; the card's palette). Could a frame belong to another film? Redo it. **The surface test:** for each still, name the texture on its largest surface in words; "none", "flat colour" or "clean low-poly" means old CG (`looks.md` §5): draw it again on the card's route before the user sees it. For a realistic look this is the realistic-render standard (`looks.md` §5): real scanned textures/HDRI first (`asset_search.py search --type texture` / `--type hdri`), and the heaviest scene's test still reports its render time (near or under a second a frame at full size, else cut the costly effect without lowering the look); every scene meets the bar, not the first worlds alone. Write what you see as numbers and fix it before the user sees it ("başlık kenara 40 px, %8 pay lazım"; "figür kadrajın %6'sı, %20 olmalı"). Then the sheet goes to the user (`SendUserFile`, render): the two-scene sheet with its question (above); every later pass's sheet with one line in the user's language, no question, while the build goes on; never only your own eyes.

**The first two scenes are the test:** a drawing that still looks wrong after two attempts (a figure, a face, a hand) changes how it is shown (silhouette, distance, an object, the look's stylisation), with one line to the user; never a third attempt at the same drawing.

**The user's own product gets its own pass** (the thing an ad sells, or an object the user names: a watch, a bottle, a machine): before the scenes, build it once in detail and show it as a full-size still beside its best subject reference. Any other object at the heart of the film (one a spark or a draw brought, a historical piece, a prop) is built in the film's medium at the detail its shots need, never measured piece by piece from a reference, unless the user asks for that fidelity. Real proportions; bevels on every edge (nothing meets at a hard right angle); the small parts that say "real" (screws, jewels, engraving, printed marks, stitching, knurling); materials told apart by roughness and colour (polished, brushed, plated, glass, leather); and light the material can show: metal and glass mirror light sources, so they get long soft strip lights and a dark field (the `glass-product` recipe's studio), not one lamp. The speed rules (a first look within minutes) are for the scenes, not for the hero: this pass may take 10–15 minutes, said in one line. A hero that reads as primitives (a cylinder for the bottle, a disc for the cap, a flat ring for the collar) is redone before any scene uses it.

**A character gets its model sheet first** (`characters.md`): the cast designed in `CAST`, the sheet rendered and sent with the first stills, before any scene uses it.

**A one-shot film is one scene:** the world built once, the camera on `CAM.path`, the beats inside the move; the rule below is for cut films. **Every scene a new picture:** a drawing used in more than one scene changes its angle, place or action each time; a new size alone is the same picture again. `qc.py repeats` measures the plain repeats.

- **Deterministic:** each scene is `{ from, to, draw(g, k, t, S), action, amb }`; `k` local progress 0–1, `t` film time, from the frame index only. Randomness only through `rng(salt)`, `noise1`, `hash1`, called inside `draw`; seeded data built in `lazy()`; nothing carried between frames.
- **The subject acts:** each scene's `action` names what the subject does; the camera moves with it, never instead of it (`qc.py action`).
- **Motion that lives** (`motion.md`): one thing leads and the rest answer; cause before effect; entrances ease out, exits ease in; attached parts follow 1–4 frames late; landings sit on the `CUES`. `MOTION` (`at`, `back`, `anticipate`, `spring`, `stagger`, `arc`) for things and words, `EASE` for the camera.
- **Style serves the subject:** heavy stylisation (grain, halftone, glitch, paint) on backgrounds and secondary layers; the subject keeps a clean silhouette and reads in one second.
- **One timeline for picture and sound:** every hit, cut and reveal is a `CUES` time (on the `BPM` grid when there is music), used by both `SCENES` and `score()`.
- **Hook:** scene 1 acts on frame 0 and the camera moves with it (ease-out, so the start is the fastest part).
- **Named camera and transitions** (`camera-moves.md`): each scene function opens with its camera line as a comment and sets `CAM` (pan, tilt, dolly, zoom, truck, crane, orbit, dollyZoom, pushTo, fpv, handheld, rackFocus, matchFrom); depth from `plane(g, S, cam, z, draw)`; time effects with `retime()`; the incoming scene's `in` picks the transition (cut, smash, match, dissolve, wipe, whip).
- **Renderer: the film and the machine choose it.** With a GPU (the setup probe names one), a world of light, air, water or terrain goes on the GPU: a fragment-shader world (ray-marched terrain and water, volumetric light and fog, depth of field, halation, real grain; `KIT/starter/index.html`) or three.js with lit materials (below), built on the kit's chain, never a bare renderer: copy `KIT/starter/looks.mjs` into the film and use `buildLook` (tone mapping and anti-aliasing by default, off only for exact colours or hard pixels; bloom, depth of field, grain, vignette by choice), `skyLight` for the sky's fill and soft shadows. Each scene takes the finishing devices that serve it and leaves off the ones that would hurt it (`references/finish.md`: 35, each with where it lifts and where it hurts), named on the card with the reason; `look.toggle('bloom', false)` and `look.focus(4.2)` set them shot by shot. Canvas 2D is for drawn, printed and graphic media and for motion design: in perspective (`VIEW`) whenever the camera looks along or around something and in flat planes only for frontal staging (the card's Camera line). **Solids seen in perspective** (buildings, streets, vehicles, boats, terrain, rooms, products) take a route the card names for this film, with no default: a drawn or printed medium in Canvas perspective (whichever suits the idea) with its marks on every face, or three.js with directional light, shadows, materials, fog and the look's finishing (`KIT/starter/looks.mjs`). Either way the light has a source and nothing realistic is shown whole in clean, even light. Plain flat-shaded polygons are never the route, whatever the reason given ("the kit's own engine" included). For lit 3D solids, many objects or real shading, three.js from the pinned CDN (`<script type="importmap">` with `three@0.186.1`, as in `KIT/starter/scene.html`), `preserveDrawingBuffer: true`, rendered inside `window.renderFrame(i)`; or a fragment shader as in `KIT/starter/index.html`. Everything loads before `window.READY`.
- **Words on screen:** `text()` cards of ≤ 6 words inside the safe area (9:16: top 12 %, bottom 20 % and right 10 % clear), readable on a phone; `label()` lower thirds for names that matter (a place, product, person), checked with `İĞÜŞÖÇ ÉÅ Ñ`.
- **Finishing, from the card's Doku line:** `FINISH` carries what the card names; `shutter: { deg: 180, samples: 8 }` (6 for ads) on `blur: true` scenes (test one scene with and without first), `weave: 0.6`, `tone: { lift: 0.035, knee: 0.72, contrast: 0.12, split: 0.02 }`.
- **Sound in the same file, from version 1** (`sound.md` §0; §8 before writing `score()`: the card's genre, progression and groove with the toolkit; §5 before writing the `SFX` table: designed sounds are accents for the moments that carry meaning, a whoosh is a low swell, at most 2 per 30 s, never a sound on every cut; `make.py` counts them): `window.renderAudio()` renders an ambience bed per scene (`amb`), picture-locked effects on the `CUES` (`SFX`: hit, whoosh, riser, tick, foley), one shared space (`ROOM`, `wet()`) and the music (`score()`). One signature sound; a scripted silence before the payoff when it helps; new voices are written in the same style as the starter's.
- **Real instruments for the tune and the hits:** `python3 tools/samples.py get cellos-pizz darbuka` (CC0, 87 players by family in `samples.py list`; only the named ones are fetched, cached once; past 50 MB for a film the tool stops and the user is asked first), then `INSTRUMENTS` and `sample()` in `score()`; pads, drones and risers stay synthesised. Leads are plucked or low-passed with a soft attack (≤ 2.4 kHz, 40 ms); no raw saw or square leads, nor bright synthetic winds or brass (`qc.py sceneloud` reports the 2–5 kHz share per scene).
- **Arrangement:** `ARRANGEMENT` lists sections × parts across the film (`spans('pad')`, `beats('kick')`); `checkArrangement()` stops overlaps and gaps. Every section has a reason to change.
- **Narration in the timeline:** `VO` rows `{ id, at, dur }` with `dur` measured from each WAV; beats are timed around the lines; the score ducks about 10 dB under every line by itself; hits and the signature sit in the gaps.

Clicking the page in a browser plays picture and sound together for a preview.

## 4. Draft first, final after the OK

**Narration, when there is one:** a helper renders candidate takes of one line as separate short files (`tts.py --audition`, `sound.md` §4b step 0) and stops; they reach the user with the first stills and the user picks by ear. Engine: Gemini TTS through `tools/tts.py` with a style prompt for type and pace (key: `keys.py list`, else `keys.py open GEMINI_API_KEY`); ElevenLabs optional. **Count before the whole narration:** one request per line, and a free Gemini key allows only a few a minute and about ten a day; with more lines than the quota, say it at the voice pick and offer fewer, longer lines (one per beat group), EMA Lightning, or billing (`tts.py` waits out a per-minute limit and stops with QUOTA SPENT and the lines left on a daily one). Without a key, Turkish narration offers EMA Lightning as one choice in the voice question (free and offline, one voice; about 800 MB once, installed only if picked: `tts.py --install ema`, then `--engine ema`), beside the OS voice as a stand-in, said so (`say --data-format=LEI16@48000 -o audio/vo/VO1.wav "…"`, Linux `espeak-ng -w …`). One WAV per line in `audio/vo/`, each measured (`ffprobe`) into `VO`. Before the mix every line gets the film's voice: `python3 tools/voice_treat.py apply audio/vo/*.wav --medium <…> --space <…> [--imprint audio/sig.wav] --out audio/vo-t`, and `audio/mix.json` points at `audio/vo-t/` (the `at` values are the `VO` table's) (`{"duration": …, "out": "audio/mix-v1.wav", "layers": [{"file": "audio/score.wav", "at": 0, "bus": "music"}, {"file": "audio/vo-t/VO1.wav", "at": <VO1.at>, "bus": "dialogue"}, …]}`); `make.py` mixes and masters it: the voice ends about 10–15 dB above the music while speaking; listen once on a phone speaker for every word. The treatment (medium in space) is the card's line, auditioned (`voice_treat.py audition`) only when the user asks.

**Half size, never half detail.** Every world is built at its full detail before the first draft, never as a blockout to be dressed later: the subject's real parts, named, its materials and surfaces, two or more secondary motions (what else moves in that world), near, middle and far layers, and how its light falls (shadow, reflection, glow where a source shines). The draft is smaller and skips the finishing passes; what is in the frame is already the final film's.

**Sound is built like the picture, before the first draft** (`sound.md` §5): go through the scenes and list every on-screen event in `review/sound-audit.md` (time · event · its sound · layers, and the line `events N · sounded M · silent by choice K`); none is left silent unless the silence is a choice written there. Each sound comes from where it sounds best (`sound.md` §5, Where each sound comes from): designed and short sounds (a word that slams, a click, glass, wood, a page, a step) from `sounds.py search` and `get`, long real recordings (a room, rain, a machine) from `asset_search.py search --type sound`, both laid on the `sfx` or `foley` bus of `audio/mix.json` at the event's frame; code only for sounds that follow the motion as it happens, land a whoosh or riser exactly on a cut, or sit in the score's key. Each place gets its bed on the `ambience` bus. The music carries the motion between events; whooshes and risers stay accents (the ceiling below).

**Draft:** `python3 tools/make.py draft` (half size, no finishing, the full sound; `--sound-only` when every note is about sound). In the background (the shell tool's `run_in_background`), `python3 tools/make.py wait` waits for that run and prints its answer; never a `pgrep` loop on make.py, which finds itself. It renders, masters to −14 LUFS, runs the checks and answers in four lines plus one per alert. Its gates do the policing: it holds back a draft whose sound is SOUND SLOP (too many whooshes or risers, a designed sound on almost every cut) or SOUND THIN (fewer than about one event sound every 4 s, or no `review/sound-audit.md`, or an audit that leaves events silent) or SOUND ALL CODE (not one ready or recorded effect, unless the card makes the film synthetic by choice; or a code score where the format's music is a recording) or SOUND LATE (a recorded effect heard 40 ms or more after its frame, from the silence at the start of its file), or that is TOO STILL, TOO REPETITIVE, EVEN RHYTHM, TOO DARK (near black almost everywhere with no bright stretch: on a phone that is mud; cinematic is not the same as dark), BLANK FRAMES (0.3 s or more with nothing on screen inside the film), AI PALETTE (over 30 % of the film on a blue-to-violet ground), TEXT (two lines stacked over each other whose letters touch, an İ dot in the line above included, or type whose letters do not stand out from what is around them) or, in an ad, has a LONG END CARD (over 4 s) for one fix pass on exactly those windows (the next draft delivers what remains); it refuses a new draft until the user's notes are in `notes/`; it stops on a dash in the film's words. It also names, without holding: NARRATION LATE (a film over a minute whose first line starts after 2 s), MUSIC WITHOUT A BEAT (a lively card over a score with no pulse) and the players of your last films again; each is fixed with the next notes, or told to the user with the draft in one plain line (what, where, why it was left) for them to decide; never only a line on the card. A long film renders in the background (`run_in_background`, then `python3 tools/make.py wait`). A loop: `python3 tools/qc.py loop renders/draft-vN.mp4`. After the first draft, one silent sentence from the sheet alone on what the film is about; a miss is the first known item. **Every per-scene flag** (a quiet or loud scene, a harsh band, a still stretch, a peak) is fixed with the notes or named as intentional in `STATE.md` (the quiet before the payoff); never a silent fix loop before the user has seen the draft.

**Deliver at once:** the film and its poster sent first (`SendUserFile`, render, as `make.py` prints them; the film plays in the side panel), the weakest moment in one line, other flags as "biliniyor, sonraki turda düzelecek", and the listening note (you measured but cannot hear: headphones once, a phone speaker once, the seconds that matter).

**The independent viewer.** Right after delivering a draft, start one helper in the background (`templates/quick/REVIEWER.md`): it took no part in the film, sees only the finished draft, its frames and its numbers, and writes `review/critique-vN.md` (the three biggest problems, each with its time, the frame that shows it and the smallest change that fixes it; red items). Do not wait for it. When the user answers, read it first: red items are fixed before any final, the rest join the user's notes as the viewer's. `make.py final` checks this itself: the newest draft's report is waited for up to 2 minutes and the final holds without it; red items hold the final until a later draft's report clears them (`--force` only when the user asks for the final without the report, or keeps a red item).

**Then always the delivery question,** never left out and never as plain text: with the question tool, in the Claude Code desktop app too (its `wrap_up` box shows two buttons and drops the rest): "Final kalitede render alayım mı ★" · "Notlarım var" · "Jüri turu" (six pinpoint questions and three fixes from the draft's own sheet, two or three minutes, `jury.md`); notes are typed in "Other".

**Final:** `python3 tools/make.py final` (determinism check, full size, the chosen finishing, checks again, `<Title>.mp4` at the project's top). Another aspect: `make.py final --size 1080x1080` delivers `<Title>-1x1.mp4` beside it (9:16 1080x1920 · 16:9 1920x1080 · 1:1 · 4:5 1080x1350). Then the two questions of `SKILL.md` Hard rules: versions without clutter. For a step `make.py` does not cover: `capture.mjs` (drafts `--maxrate 4M`, half size, `shutter=0&weave=0&tone=0`), `master_audio.py <wav> <film.mp4> --video <picture.mp4> --limit` (it must print `normalization_type=linear` and OK), `qc.py all | sheet | thumb` (each `--help`).

## 7. Long films (up to about 3 minutes): one film in chapters

Chapters of 15–30 s are an internal structure for edits and renders; the viewer sees one film.

- **One arc:** each chapter is caused by the one before and the film builds to its ending; a turn, when the story has one, happens once.
- **One visual language:** the same recipe, palette family, type, texture and camera rules; light may develop, never jump.
- **Motifs travel:** a motif introduced early returns transformed; tracked in `STATE.md`.
- **Seams connect:** a match cut, motion carried over the cut, a morph of the motif or a J/L-cut; no unmotivated hard breaks; a smash cut only where the story asks for the shock.
- **One music track** on one `BPM` grid; sections (intro, build, peak, release) change on bar lines; the motif returns changed at the end.
- **Beats by chapter** at the gate, as one flow (the first progress stills come from four chapters and should look like one film): `C1 0:00–0:20 <what happens> · motif: <the motif> · <the camera line> · → C2 <the seam>`.
- **Variety within unity:** adjacent shots vary scale, tempo, move and text treatment; every chapter has an event; the big moves (dolly zoom, freeze, smash cut, speed ramp) once each at most.
- **Unity check:** watch every seam at 1× with sound (`python3 tools/qc.py strip renders/film-v1.mp4 <seam − 1> 2 6`); a seam that reads as a new clip gets a better transition or the motif across it.
- **One file, per chapter:** scene functions named `c3_bridge`, `SCENES` rows with `chapter: 'C3'`, chapter starts in `CUES`; a note on chapter 3 edits only `c3_*`.
- **Render time:** Canvas 2D runs at about 20–60 frames a second on a recent Mac, so 3 minutes (4,320 frames) takes minutes; heavy three.js chapters 10–30. Drafts at half size; finals in the background.

## 8. Saved formats: the same language again

A film the user liked can become a format: the next film speaks its language about a new idea. A format is never a copy.

- **Save** (on request, after the final): `python3 tools/formats.py save "<name>" --rhythm … --hook … --ending …` keeps the source film's files and writes `FORMAT.md`; fill its language lines from the card (look and medium, palette logic, type style, camera and form, rhythm, hook, ending, sound language, atmosphere), each as a way of making the film, not a value, and naming no object, word or image of the source film: those are its content. One question before saving: does anything return in every film as the format's signature (★ none · the closing image · the opening move · a sound · own)? Each pick is saved with `--keep "<the signature>"` in its own section; only those return.
- **Use:** `formats.py list` → `python3 KIT/scripts/init_film.py <short-name> --quick --format <name>`: the new film starts from the fresh template with `FORMAT.md`, and the source film's code only as `research/format-reference.html` (to look up how a technique was drawn). Ask only the idea, and the length when it differs; when the format's sound language names a recording, one more question for the music (★ a new track in that genre and tempo range, three previews, `sound.md` §8 "A real recording"), never a score written in code in its place.
- **The card** writes each language line again for the new subject and says how it meets it ("palette logic: <the rule>, here <how this film meets it>"); the type is the same face or a sibling of the same voice; the hex values may change by the same logic.
- **Always new:** the story, every word, every scene and image (the hook's subject, every shape and the ending's image come from this idea; the source film's own images never return, unless FORMAT.md keeps one on purpose as the signature), CUES and SFX, and the music itself: a new progression, motif and arrangement in the same genre, tempo range and family of players. `make.py draft` holds a draft whose score, scenes or cues are the source film's again (FORMAT COPY).
